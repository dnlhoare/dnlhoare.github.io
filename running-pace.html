How to read these estimates

Race times use the Riegel power law: T₂ = T₁ × (D₂ / D₁)1.06. It is a simple equivalency model for endurance efforts, so predictions become less reliable the farther the target is from your result. Sprint estimates (100–800 m) are especially rough.

VDOT uses the Daniels–Gilbert oxygen cost and sustainable-fraction equations, and is only shown for efforts from 1500 m to marathon. It is a performance index sometimes called “effective” VO₂max; it does not equal a measured laboratory VO₂max. Age and weight scenarios are illustrative, broad assumptions. Marathon outcomes also depend heavily on preparation and fueling. Race elevation is shown as context and does not alter VDOT or the chart; prediction elevation uses a simplified balanced uphill/downhill assumption.

Sources: Riegel, “Athletic Records and Human Endurance” · Daniels–Gilbert VDOT equations · Minetti et al., running energy cost on slopes · Review of age related VO₂ changes.
